# Deload Prompt — Methodology

A deload is not a punishment for being weak; it's the recovery half
of the stress-recovery-adaptation cycle. Programs that don't include
them eventually stall, regress, or break the lifter.

## Recognition signals — when the user needs a deload

Most intermediate-and-up lifters earn a deload every 4–6 weeks of
hard training. Outside that schedule, watch for signal clusters
(any 2+ for 5–7 days):

1. **Three or more failed working sets** at weights / reps that
   were comfortable two weeks ago.
2. **Sleep disruption** — falling asleep fine but waking at 3–4am
   wired, or trouble falling asleep on training nights.
3. **Joint soreness in places that aren't usually sore** —
   elbow tendons, knee tracks, AC joints. Not muscle DOMS.
4. **Resting heart rate elevated** by 5+ bpm for several mornings
   in a row, with no obvious cause (illness, dehydration, alcohol).
5. **Motivation crash** — gym dread the user normally doesn't
   have. Workouts feel heavy on the walk in, not just under the
   bar.
6. **Plateau on rep PRs**, not just weight PRs. Volume PRs are
   usually the last to stall; when they do, it's a fatigue signal.
7. **Loss of pump / pump that takes much longer to come on.**
   Reflects accumulated central fatigue.

One signal alone is noise. Two or three together is signal.

## Deload styles — pick by what's burning

### Volume cut (most common)

- Cut working sets per muscle group by ~40–50% for the week.
- Keep load on the bar at ~80–90% of the previous week's working
  weight.
- Keep movement quality high — same lifts, same RPE 6–7 sets, just
  fewer of them.
- Best when the user is fatigued from accumulated volume but their
  joints feel fine and their nervous system isn't fried.

### Intensity cut

- Keep the volume (sets × reps) similar.
- Cut load to ~60–70% of working weight, ceiling at RPE 7.
- Best when joints are barking — high-frequency lifters often need
  this style after a peaking block.
- The lifter still feels like they trained; the bar just isn't
  trying to hurt them.

### Full rest week

- No lifting, no high-intensity conditioning. Walks, easy bike,
  mobility, sleep.
- Best when the user has 4+ recognition signals firing, illness,
  major life stress, or coming off a meet / event.
- Counter-intuitive but: most lifters underuse this and overuse
  the other two. A real rest week is sometimes the best
  performance enhancer in a year.

## What to keep doing during a deload

- **Sleep on schedule.** Deload weeks are when the body actually
  banks the adaptation work — protect sleep harder, not less.
- **Eat at maintenance**, not in deficit. Underfeeding a deload
  defeats it.
- **Walk daily** — 7–10k steps. Recovery loves blood flow.
- **Mobility work** the user usually skips. Hips, T-spine,
  shoulders.
- **Skill work** at very low load if the user is technique-driven
  (olympic lifters, powerlifters working on a specific cue).

## What to stop doing

- Hitting RPE 9–10 anywhere. The point is recovery; if it feels
  like a normal training week, it isn't a deload.
- Adding a "fun" novel-stimulus session. Novelty is fatigue
  too — sled pushes, hill sprints, yoke walks all count.
- Tracking food obsessively. If the user has tendencies that way,
  this is the week to loosen the leash.

## De-deload return protocol

The week after the deload is not a PR week. It's the bridge.

- **Week +1** — return to ~85–90% of the pre-deload working weight
  for the same sets × reps. Should feel easy. If it doesn't, the
  deload wasn't deep enough; consider another short deload.
- **Week +2** — match or slightly exceed the pre-deload best (rep
  PR or weight PR, lifter's choice).
- **Week +3 onward** — resume normal progression.

The most common deload mistake is rushing back to PR territory in
week +1. Adaptation isn't fully on the books yet; the body has
banked recovery, not super-compensation.

## Output

Emit the deload as a `:::artifact` block with:

- Recognition signals observed.
- Style chosen (volume cut / intensity cut / full rest) and why.
- Adjusted week's sessions in the same shape as the user's normal
  program (so it's executable, not abstract).
- The de-deload return prescription for week +1.
