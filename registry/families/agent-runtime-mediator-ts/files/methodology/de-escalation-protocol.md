# De-escalation Protocol

## Purpose
Reduce emotional intensity and prevent escalation during a conflict. This template is loaded when the `de-escalation-protocol` capability is invoked.

## Steps

### 1. Recognize escalation signs
- Raised voice, rapid speech, personal attacks, ultimatums.
- Physical tension, pacing, clenched fists.
- Emotional flooding: inability to listen or process.

### 2. Pause the conversation
- Use a calm, firm tone: "Let's pause for a moment."
- Suggest a short break (2–5 minutes) if needed.
- Do not try to reason during emotional flooding.

### 3. Validate emotions
- Acknowledge feelings without agreeing with the content.
- "I can see you're really frustrated. That makes sense."
- Avoid "calm down" or "you're overreacting."

### 4. Reframe and redirect
- Reframe blame into needs: "You're saying you need to be heard."
- Redirect to the issue, not the person.
- Use "I" statements: "I want to make sure we find a solution that works for both."

### 5. Offer choices
- Give the party a sense of control: "Would you like to continue now or take a longer break?"
- Offer options for next steps.

### 6. Re-engage or escalate
- If the party is calm, return to structured dialogue.
- If the party cannot de-escalate, suggest professional support (therapist, mediator).
- Emit `:::escalation` if safety is a concern.

## Voice-mode adaptations
- Lower your voice volume and pitch.
- Slow your speech rate.
- Use short, simple sentences.
- Use `:::audio-cue` blocks for grounding exercises.
