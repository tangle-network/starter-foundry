# Technique Cue Template

## Purpose
Deliver a single, specific technique in a tight, actionable form.
The user has named what they're after (calm down, focus, get to
sleep, ground in panic) and the agent prescribes the right
technique with rhythm and timing they can follow.

## When to use
Trigger when the user asks "how do I calm down right now" / "I
can't sleep" / "I'm having a panic attack." For a longer guided
practice, route to guided-session.md. For a check-in, route to
mindfulness-check.md.

## Library of techniques

### Box breathing (4-4-4-4)
- **For**: pre-meeting nerves, mid-day reset, mild anxiety.
- **Counts**: inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4.
- **Duration**: 4–6 cycles (~2 minutes).
- **Cue**: "In, two, three, four. Hold, two, three, four. Out,
  two, three, four. Hold, two, three, four."
- **Adjustments**: shorten to 3-3-3-3 if dizzy; lengthen exhale
  (4-4-6-2) if more downregulation needed.

### Physiological sigh (Huberman / Stephen Porges work)
- **For**: rapid downregulation, before sleep, post-stress.
- **Pattern**: double inhale through nose (one breath, then a
  small top-up), then long exhale through mouth.
- **Duration**: 1–3 cycles is usually enough.
- **Cue**: "Inhale through the nose. Then a small extra inhale
  on top. Then a long, slow exhale through the mouth."

### 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (acute panic / dissociation)
- **For**: panic, dissociation, anxiety spike.
- **Method**: name 5 things you see, 4 you feel, 3 you hear, 2
  you smell, 1 you taste.
- **Duration**: 2–4 minutes.
- **Cue**: "Look around. Name five things you can see. Out loud
  if you can." (long pause) "Now four things you feel — your
  feet on the floor, the chair, the air on your skin, your
  hands." (continue down to 1)
- **Note**: this technique is *grounding*, not relaxation. The
  goal is presence, not calm.

### 4-7-8 breathing (Andrew Weil)
- **For**: difficulty falling asleep, persistent rumination.
- **Counts**: inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8 (with audible "whoosh").
- **Duration**: 4 cycles; can repeat after pause.
- **Cue**: "Inhale through the nose for four. Hold for seven.
  Exhale through the mouth, slowly, for eight."
- **Caution**: not for someone with respiratory conditions or
  history of fainting; use shorter counts.

### Body scan (3-minute)
- **For**: end-of-day wind-down, transition between tasks.
- **Method**: attention from feet to head, ~10 seconds per
  region.
- **Duration**: 3 minutes (longer in guided-session.md).

### Loving-kindness (1-minute)
- **For**: rumination on a person, frustration with self.
- **Method**: repeat silently four phrases — "May you be safe.
  May you be healthy. May you be at ease. May you live with
  ease." Direct first to self, then a loved one, then a neutral
  person, then someone you struggle with.

### Open-awareness rest
- **For**: post-task fatigue, depletion.
- **Method**: lie down or sit; do not concentrate on anything
  specific; let attention drift; if you notice you're "trying"
  to do anything, gently release.
- **Duration**: 5–20 minutes.

## Method (selecting a technique)

1. **Listen for the specific ask.** "Calm" is not enough — is
   the user spiraling (panic), wired (over-stimulated),
   pre-event (anxious), at bedtime (insomnia), or depleted
   (post-stress)?
2. **Match technique to need.**
   - Panic / dissociation → 5-4-3-2-1.
   - Pre-event nerves → box breathing.
   - Sleep onset → 4-7-8.
   - Mid-day reset → physiological sigh + 30 second mindful
     pause.
   - Rumination → loving-kindness or open-awareness.
3. **Confirm the user is in a safe place.**
   - Not driving, not operating machinery.
   - Box / 4-7-8 breathing should not be done while moving (mild
     dizziness possible).
4. **Deliver in the user's preferred mode.** Voice mode: speak
   the cue with rhythm; text mode: write it as a script with
   counts, then a pause, then "let me know when you'd like to
   continue."
5. **Offer one cycle, then check in.** "How does that feel?
   Want another cycle?" — don't railroad through 5 minutes.

## Voice-delivery rules

- Pace: half normal speed for cue delivery.
- Counts: clearly enunciated, evenly spaced, never rushed.
- Pause between sentences ≥2 seconds.
- Avoid: "now," "just," "simply" as filler.
- Avoid the "yoga teacher" voice — even and quiet beats
  performatively peaceful.

## Discipline rules

- **Match the technique to the state.** A box-breathing cue to
  a panicking user can intensify the panic (over-control of
  breath). 5-4-3-2-1 grounding is safer.
- **Don't push.** If a technique isn't landing, switch or stop.
- **Don't medicalize.** This isn't treatment. If the user is
  having a panic disorder, PTSD activation, or persistent
  symptoms — escalate to a clinician (route to therapist
  bundle).
- **Don't claim outcomes.** "This will calm you down" — instead
  "this often helps; let's see."

## Escalation

If during a technique the user reports:
- Suicidality / self-harm → therapist escalation-protocol.md.
- Acute medical symptoms (chest pain, severe dizziness, fainting)
  → call 911 / local emergency.
- Trauma flashback / dissociation that worsens with breath-
  focused work → switch to grounding (5-4-3-2-1), then surface
  professional support; trauma-trained therapist preferred.
