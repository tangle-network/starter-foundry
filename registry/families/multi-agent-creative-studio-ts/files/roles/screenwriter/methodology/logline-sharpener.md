# Logline sharpener

A logline is one sentence. If it takes two, it's not a logline yet.

## The structure

`When [inciting incident], [protagonist] must [external goal]
before [stakes/deadline] — but [obstacle].`

Not all four pieces always appear, but a logline missing more than
one is usually missing the story.

## Common failure modes

1. **Theme as logline.** "A meditation on grief and second
   chances." Theme is what the story is *about*. Logline is what
   *happens*.
2. **Setup as logline.** "Set in a small town in Maine, a family
   inherits a house..." That's act one. Where's the want?
3. **Generic protagonist.** "A woman" / "a young man." If the
   protagonist's identity matters to the conflict, name the thing
   that makes them specific. ("A grieving widow", "a disgraced
   pianist".)
4. **No obstacle.** "A detective solves a murder." That's a job
   description, not a story. What stands between her and the
   truth?
5. **Two protagonists.** Pick one. The other becomes part of the
   obstacle, or the goal, or the stakes — but the lens is on one.
6. **Adaptation logline collapses to "based on."** "Based on the
   novel by X." That's a credit, not a logline. The adaptation
   needs its own one-sentence story — the novel's story compressed
   to its bone.

## The compression test

Read the logline out loud. If you stumble, it's too long. If the
listener says "...and?" at the end, you missed the obstacle. If
they say "okay so what's the *story*", you have setup, not story.

## When to push back

If the writer's logline is doing thematic work the story can't pay
off (e.g., "a story about whether love can survive trauma" but the
story is actually a heist), say so. The logline is a contract with
the reader; if the script doesn't honor it, change one or the
other.

## Adaptation context

When the screenplay is adapted from a novel handed off by the
novelist-coach, the logline question is harder: the novel has its
own logline (implicit), the script has its own (which compresses,
sometimes ruthlessly).

- **Read the source's spine.** What is the novel's value-shift in
  one phrase? "From self-deception to self-knowledge." "From
  belonging to exile." That spine is what the script must carry.
- **Compress the source's plot to one sentence.** Most novels lose
  90% of their plot in adaptation. The 10% that remains must
  contain the spine.
- **The script's logline can differ from the novel's.** A novel
  whose logline is "a woman returns to her childhood home and
  remembers her father" might become a script whose logline is
  "after her father's death, a woman returns to the half-built
  lighthouse he abandoned and finds his coat — and the truth he
  ran from." Same spine; different cinematic engine.

If the adapted logline drifts from the novel's spine, surface to
the novelist-coach (`:::handoff to: novelist-coach reason: adapted
logline diverges from source's value-shift on <axis>`). The
novelist decides whether the divergence is fidelity-preserving or
fidelity-breaking.

## Reference shapes

- *Die Hard*: A New York cop visiting his estranged wife at her
  Christmas party must rescue her and the other hostages when
  terrorists seize the building.
- *Tootsie*: An unemployable actor disguises himself as a woman to
  land a soap opera role, then falls for his co-star.
- *Get Out*: A young Black man visits his white girlfriend's
  parents for the weekend and discovers their hospitality hides a
  horrifying secret.

Each is one sentence. Each names protagonist, want, obstacle.
Each promises a story.

## What goes in the artifact

```
:::artifact role=screenwriter template=logline-sharpener id=logline-2026-04-26 title="Logline pass — <project-slug>"

## Their draft
<the writer's current logline, verbatim>

## What's working
<one sentence — the part you don't touch>

## What's not yet
<the missing piece: protagonist specificity, obstacle, stakes,
or compression>

## Three rewrites (pick one or write your own)
1. <variant A — minimal change, sharpens what's there>
2. <variant B — restructures around a different protagonist beat>
3. <variant C — wildcard — what if the obstacle is X instead of Y>

## The test
Read it out loud. Where do you stumble? That's the soft spot.
:::
```
